How to Write a Cover Letter: Structure and Fill-In Template

A good cover letter is short, specific and about the employer’s needs rather than a repeat of your resume. Use the structure below, keep it to about half a page to one page, and tailor the middle paragraph for each application.

The four-paragraph structure

  1. Opening: the role you are applying for, where you saw it, and one sentence on why you are a strong fit.
  2. Proof: one or two achievements that match what the job asks for, with a result or number where you honestly have one.
  3. Why them: something specific about the company, team or product that interests you.
  4. Close: thank them, say you would welcome a conversation, and sign off with your contact details.

Fill-in template

Dear [Hiring Manager name],

I am writing to apply for the [Job title] role at [Company], which I found on [source]. With [X years / relevant background] in [field], I am excited about the chance to contribute to [specific goal or project].

In my most recent role at [Company], I [achievement with result]. This is directly relevant to your need for [requirement from the posting].

I am drawn to [Company] because [specific reason]. I would welcome the chance to discuss how I can help your team with [goal].

Thank you for your time and consideration.

Kind regards,
[Name] · [Phone] · [Email]

Changing careers?

Lead with transferable skills and a clear reason for the move. Pick the two or three requirements in the posting you can already meet, and describe the matching work from your past roles in the employer’s language.

Send a short follow-up

If you have not heard back after a week or two, a brief, polite follow-up email restating your interest and attaching nothing new is normal and professional. Keep it to three or four sentences.

FAQ

How long should a cover letter be?

About half a page to one page, three to four short paragraphs. Hiring managers skim, so make every sentence earn its place.

Do I still need a cover letter if the application does not require one?

If there is an optional field, a short tailored letter can help you stand out, especially for career changes or roles where your resume does not tell the whole story.

Should I use the same cover letter for every job?

Keep the structure, but change the opening, the proof paragraph and the reason you want that company each time.
