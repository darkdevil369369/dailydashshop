ATS-Friendly Resume Checklist: 12 Fixes Before You Apply

Most mid-size and large employers run applications through an applicant tracking system (ATS) that turns your file into plain text before a person ever reads it. If your layout confuses that parser, your best experience can end up scrambled or missing. This checklist covers the formatting and wording fixes that matter most.

What an ATS actually does

An ATS extracts text from your resume, sorts it into fields (name, employers, dates, skills) and lets recruiters search and filter. It does not judge design. The goal is not to impress software, it is to make sure your information survives the conversion intact so a human can read it.

The 12-point checklist

  1. Use one column. Multi-column and sidebar layouts can be read out of order.
  2. Use standard section headings such as Experience, Education and Skills rather than creative labels.
  3. Keep contact details in the body, not only in the header or footer, where some parsers skip them.
  4. Avoid tables, text boxes and graphics for core content.
  5. Never put text inside images, including logos with your name or skill bars with ratings.
  6. Use a common font at a readable size (10 to 12 pt).
  7. List jobs in reverse-chronological order with title, company and consistent dates (for example Jan 2022 – Mar 2025).
  8. Mirror the job description honestly. If the posting says “project management” and you did that work, use that phrase rather than a synonym.
  9. Spell out acronyms once with the abbreviation in brackets, for example Search Engine Optimization (SEO).
  10. Use simple bullets (a plain dot or hyphen) and start each with an action verb.
  11. Submit the format the employer asks for. A .docx or a text-based PDF is usually safe; avoid scanned or image-only PDFs.
  12. Name the file clearly, for example firstname-lastname-resume.pdf, and proofread for typos that break keyword matches.

Keywords: match the job, do not stuff

Read the posting and note the hard skills, tools, certifications and job titles it repeats. Work the ones you genuinely have into your summary, skills list and bullet points. Repeating a keyword dozens of times does not help and reads badly to the recruiter who opens the file next.

Test your resume before you send it

Paste your resume into a plain-text editor. If sections appear in the wrong order or characters turn into symbols, a parser will likely struggle too. You can also run it through our free resume checker for a quick formatting and keyword review.

FAQ

Do I need a plain, boring resume to pass an ATS?

No. A clean, single-column design with a little accent colour parses fine. What causes problems is layout trickery: columns, tables, text boxes and text embedded in images.

Is a PDF or a Word file better for ATS?

Follow the instructions in the job posting. When it does not say, a .docx or a text-based PDF exported from your editor is generally safe. Avoid image-only or scanned PDFs.

Should I use the exact words from the job description?

Use the same wording where it truthfully describes your experience, since many systems search by keyword. Do not add skills you do not have.
