How to Write a Freelance Invoice: What to Include

A clear invoice gets paid faster. Most late payments come down to missing details, vague terms or an invoice that was never sent to the right person. Here is what a professional freelance invoice contains and how to word the parts clients tend to question.

What to include on every invoice

  • The word “Invoice” and a unique invoice number so you and your client can both refer to it.
  • Issue date and due date.
  • Your details: name or business name, address, email and any business or tax ID that applies where you operate.
  • Client details: the legal name and billing contact of whoever is paying.
  • Line items: a short description, quantity or hours, rate and amount for each deliverable.
  • Subtotal, taxes if applicable, and total due with the currency stated.
  • Payment instructions: how and where to pay (bank transfer, card link, PayPal or similar).
  • Payment terms: for example “Net 14” or “Due on receipt”.

Tax and invoicing rules differ by country and by region, so check what applies to you or ask an accountant.

Choosing payment terms

Short terms such as Net 7 or Net 14 keep cash flow healthy; Net 30 is common with larger companies. Whatever you choose, agree it in your proposal or contract before work starts and repeat it on the invoice. For new clients, consider a deposit or milestone payments so you are never carrying a large unpaid balance.

Late fees and reminders

If you charge a late fee, state it in the contract and on the invoice (for example a flat fee or a monthly percentage, within what is legal where you work). A simple reminder sequence works well: a friendly note a few days before the due date, a reminder on the due date, then a firmer follow-up a week later that references the original terms.

FAQ

What is the difference between an invoice and a receipt?

An invoice requests payment for work done or about to be done. A receipt confirms that payment has been received.

Should I invoice before or after the work is finished?

Both are common. Many freelancers take a deposit up front, invoice milestones during long projects and send the final invoice on delivery. Agree the schedule in writing first.

How do I number my invoices?

Any consistent, unique sequence works, such as 0001, 0002 or a year-based format like 2026-014. Do not reuse or skip numbers without a reason.
