About NOCT

Old questions, drawn plainly.

NOCT exists to explain the strange things minds do, and the strange things our ancestors did, in plain English with a drawing for every idea.

The mission

A lot of popular psychology is repeated long after the evidence has moved on. Named effects get quoted as laws, and "our ancestors did X" gets said with a confidence the historical record does not support. NOCT picks those claims up one at a time and checks them: what the original study found, what happened when others tried to repeat it, and what the sources actually say.

The goal is simple. You should finish an episode understanding the idea better than the version you heard before, including the parts that are still uncertain.

The format

Full episodes
About nine minutes, one question each, released every Tuesday and Friday on YouTube.
Shorts
Under a minute, a single idea cut from an episode, released on the other five days on YouTube Shorts and TikTok.
Topics
Psychology and named effects (the Zeigarnik effect, the Ringelmann effect and others) and "what ancient humans actually did": sleep, work, food and daily life before the modern world.
Style
One fixed hand-drawn stick-figure style on a cream page, with a single narrator and a quiet music bed. No stock footage, no borrowed clips, no burned-in captions.
Behind the episode

How an episode is made

  1. A topic that argues with itself

    A topic only makes the list if it carries a documented contradiction: a famous finding that failed to replicate, a common belief the historical record disagrees with, or two studies that point opposite ways. If there is no tension in the sources, there is no episode.

  2. Research and citation check

    The script is written from the primary literature. Every study it cites is checked against Crossref (for the DOI and publication record) and PubMed (for the title and abstract) before anything is drawn. Claims that cannot be traced to a source are cut. Our first episode changed direction at this stage, when a large 2025 meta-analysis showed the effect it described does not reliably replicate.

  3. Script

    About 1,700 words, written to be heard rather than read: short sentences, one idea per beat, and the contradiction set up early so the payoff lands at the end.

  4. Drawing

    The script is split into roughly 170 beats, and each one gets its own illustration in the same hand-drawn stick-figure style: black line, cream paper, mustard sun, slate blue ground. The art style never changes between episodes; only the subject does.

  5. Narration and edit

    A single, steady narrator voice reads the finished script. The drawings are cut to the narration, one new image every few seconds with a slow camera move, over a music bed kept well below the voice.

  6. Release

    The finished episode is reviewed and scheduled for the next Tuesday or Friday slot. Shorts are cut from it for the days in between and published to YouTube Shorts and, through our own NOCT Publisher tool, to the NOCT TikTok account.
